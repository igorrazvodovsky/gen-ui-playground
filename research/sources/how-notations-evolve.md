## Core idea

Notations are socially constructed representational systems that evolve through three stages: invention & incubation, dispersion & divergence, and institutionalisation & sanctification. The paper identifies 33 patterns across these stages, organised into four categories (cognitive, social, formal, constraint). The central mechanism: notation definition proceeds by mapping **dimensions of meaningful variation** in a conceptual domain to **dimensions of perceptually distinguishable variation** in a representational domain. This mapping is mediated by linking metaphors (analogical transfers from existing notations) and grounding metaphors (connections to embodied experience).

## Key concepts

- [[dimensions-of-meaningful-variation]] — the core mechanism of notation design: foregrounding what matters in the domain, backgrounding or excluding everything else
- [[linking-and-grounding-metaphors]] — two types of conceptual metaphor that drive notation invention and constrain its evolution
- [[notation-evolution-stages]] — three-stage social lifecycle (invention → dispersion → institutionalisation) with distinct patterns at each stage
- [[translation-vs-creation-in-AI]] — the paper's closing argument that current AI supports horizontal formalisation (translation to existing notations) but not vertical creation of new ones

## Technical approach

Comparative historical analysis across five disciplines (music, dance, chemistry, physics, computer programming), later expanded to sign language writing, UML, Markdown, juggling notation, and quantum circuits. The methodology follows parallel comparative history (Theda Skocpol): broad case coverage, inductive iteration between data and theory, saturation-driven expansion. Patterns are clustered into the most relevant social stage and tagged with category badges (Cognitive, Social, Formal, Constraint). The paper also includes a worked example — an in-progress notation for human-AI "common ground" using Venn diagrams — that demonstrates the cognitive patterns in real-time.

No technical implementation. No evaluation beyond the comparative analysis itself. This is a theory paper.
